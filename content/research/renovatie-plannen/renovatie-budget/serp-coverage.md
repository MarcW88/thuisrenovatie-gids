## Search intent

The dominant task is not merely to estimate what renovation work costs. The reader wants to know how much can safely be spent, how to divide that money, how much uncertainty to retain, and what to cut or postpone when offers exceed the available amount.

This page therefore owns allocation and control. Current market prices belong to `/renovatie-plannen/renovatiekosten/`; financing products belong to specialist finance guidance.

## Current SERP observations

- Vereniging Eigen Huis explicitly asks homeowners to determine the total budget and decide in advance what falls away if an offer exceeds that budget.
- VEH separates structural changes from aesthetic improvements and also treats financing as a distinct decision.
- VEH warns that `stelposten` are estimated amounts whose final cost is not yet known; too many or unclear provisional sums reduce budget certainty.
- Nationale-Nederlanden (updated 1 July 2026) recommends clarifying the scope, requesting several offers and allowing a practical 10–20% extra buffer for unexpected costs.
- NN separately advises homeowners not to spend their entire personal cash buffer on the renovation: a project contingency and the household's wider emergency buffer serve different purposes.
- Funda's 2025 consumer guidance uses a 10–15% contingency and stresses prioritisation, realistic estimates and comparable offers. It is useful secondary guidance, not a technical norm.

## SHOULD coverage

- Distinguish `budgetplafond`, `begroting`, `vastgelegd`, `verwacht`, `vrij`, and `reserve` in plain Dutch.
- Do not create a universal percentage allocation for fixed scope/options/project costs.
- Show how a €80,000 example evolves after a higher quote or newly discovered work, rather than presenting one static split.
- Explain that a signed quote does not necessarily make every amount fixed when `stelposten`, quantities or change work remain open.
- Keep financing options concise and route outward; this page should not become a mortgage/loan guide.

## Information gain

1. **Five-number budget dashboard:** total ceiling, committed (`vastgelegd`), expected but not committed (`verwacht`), contingency (`reserve`), and still free (`vrij`).
2. **Two-buffer rule:** renovation contingency is separate from the household's own emergency buffer.
3. **Priority ladder:** `must / should / could` choices are agreed before offers arrive, so overruns trigger a decision rather than panic.
4. **Uncertainty register:** provisional sums, unknown quantities, unresolved technical questions and possible subsidies remain visible as uncertain instead of being silently counted as fixed.
5. **Change-control loop:** every accepted change updates total forecast, reserve and free room immediately.
6. **Worked €80k scenario:** use the existing example as a moving budget after one quote increases and one hidden defect appears; clearly label as illustration, not recommended allocation.
