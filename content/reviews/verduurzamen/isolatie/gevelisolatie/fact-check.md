## Sources verified

### Milieu Centraal — Spouwmuurisolatie

Current page checked 13 September 2026; page states last modified 8 July 2026.

Supported claims used:

- houses built before roughly 1920 often lack a cavity wall, but build year is only a clue and not proof;
- a cavity is commonly around 4–6 cm wide;
- before cavity-wall insulation, a specialist should inspect existing insulation, debris/protruding mortar, brickwork, pointing, frames, roof/gutters and relevant surface coatings;
- cavity-wall insulation is specialist work rather than normal DIY;
- ordinary cavity-wall insulation does not itself change the exterior appearance, so Milieu Centraal states no environmental permit is needed for that measure itself;
- nature-protection duties under the Omgevingswet still apply;
- adequate house ventilation remains necessary;
- old moderate cavity insulation can sometimes be topped up or the cavity cleaned first after inspection;
- an example with about 5 cm added insulation gives around `Rd 1.2 m²K/W`, bringing a typical total wall to roughly `Rc 1.55`;
- Milieu Centraal currently advises against UF foam and states that ISDE/SVVE subsidy for it was temporarily stopped from 1 April 2026.

Boundary preserved:

- the article does not say every house after 1920 has a cavity;
- it does not say a 4 cm cavity is automatically fillable;
- it does not prescribe one cavity material for all façades.

### Milieu Centraal — Buitenmuur isoleren met voorzetwand

Current page checked 13 September 2026; page states last modified 24 August 2026.

Supported claims used:

- suitability for interior insulation depends on cavity/build-up, existing insulation, moisture problems, timber floor/ceiling beams and vapour-resisting layers;
- unresolved moisture can need treatment first;
- timber beam ends embedded in the exterior wall can become colder after internal insulation and may face condensation/rot risk;
- the location of vapour-resisting layers can determine whether/how a wall can be insulated;
- a room-side vapour-control layer is usually needed with an internal lining, but exact build-up depends on the wall;
- a typical internal lining can reduce room dimensions by roughly 10–15 cm depending on system;
- radiators, sockets, skirtings and window reveals are relevant scope items;
- `Rd 3.5` is used by Milieu Centraal as a strong performance/subsidy-relevant level for internal wall insulation while thinner insulation can still improve comfort where space is constrained.

Boundary preserved:

- no remote hygrothermal design is given;
- no claim that every wall needs the same membrane detail;
- DIY is not normalised when timber beam ends, moisture or unknown layers create uncertainty.

### Milieu Centraal — Buitenmuur isoleren aan de buitenkant

Current page checked 13 September 2026.

Supported claims used:

- cavity-wall insulation is usually much cheaper where the cavity is suitable;
- external wall insulation is especially relevant when there is no cavity, the cavity is unsuitable, major façade maintenance is needed, or a high insulation level is desired;
- an unsuitable cavity can include one narrower than about 4 cm or heavily contaminated with mortar remnants;
- external insulation can accommodate a thicker layer; Milieu Centraal recommends a high performance and gives `Rd 4.5` as a reference level;
- external insulation changes the appearance and Milieu Centraal states a permit is needed / advises checking with municipality and Omgevingsloket;
- terraced, semi-detached, listed and protected-streetscape situations can have additional constraints;
- moisture penetration can have causes such as gutters, rising damp or ventilation and may require correction before insulation;
- nature-protection rules can apply where an uninsulated cavity or other hollow façade element may host protected species;
- details around frames, roof edge/gutters and other façade transitions matter;
- current cost examples show very different orders of magnitude between cavity and external insulation.

### Milieu Centraal — Natuurbescherming in de Omgevingswet

Current page checked 13 September 2026.

Supported claims used:

- cavity spaces can host protected bats and birds;
- certified eDNA testing is an available route;
- a negative eDNA test can allow direct continuation under the applicable route;
- a positive test requires further legal/ecological handling and in many cases an environmental permit/ecological research, with provincial routes able to differ;
- therefore the article correctly treats nature as a pre-work gate rather than a footnote.

Boundary preserved:

- the article does not promise that eDNA alone authorises every façade project;
- it directs the user to current provincial/permit handling rather than hard-coding a universal process.

### Vereniging Eigen Huis — Gevelisolatie / spouwmuurisolatie

Current pages checked September 2026.

Supported secondary claims:

- brickwork, pointing and cracks should be assessed before cavity insulation;
- internal and external insulation have different consequences for room size, details, moisture and exterior appearance.

## Cost claim review

Milieu Centraal currently uses approximately:

- `€1,800` for cavity-wall insulation of a reference corner house;
- `€23,000` for external wall insulation of a reference corner house.

The article deliberately frames these as route-specific current examples, not comparable universal project quotes. It explicitly explains that exterior insulation includes a much broader scope with new outer finish and detail work.

No universal national `€/m²` figure is published.
